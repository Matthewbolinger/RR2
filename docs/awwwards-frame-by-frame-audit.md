# Awwwards Benchmark and Frame-by-Frame Jury Audit

Review date: July 30, 2026

Status: synthetic expert and homeowner review of the local RR2 build. This predicts likely reactions from the supplied evidence; it is not a substitute for observed customer research or an official Awwwards score.

## Executive verdict

Raccoon Restoration already looks substantially more premium, disciplined, and trustworthy than a typical local roofing website. The hero, service gallery, storm guidance, mobile calls to action, and plain-language service copy are the strongest work.

The current experience is not yet Site of the Year caliber. It has strong art direction, but the authored experience stops before the proof climax. The middle of the homepage repeats equal-card and ruled-grid compositions, most motion is one restrained reveal pattern, secondary pages share one hero grammar, and the most important trust destinations openly describe proof or operational facts that are not ready.

**Synthetic Awwwards-style score: 7.7/10**

| Criterion | Score | Jury reading |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Design | 8.1 | Coherent, controlled, premium, responsive, and unusually disciplined for home services |
| Usability | 8.7 | Clear intent routing, strong calls to action, usable mobile navigation, accessible-minded interaction |
| Creativity | 5.8 | Distinctive brand styling, but familiar editorial grids, shared page templates, and no signature useful interaction |
| Content | 6.8 | Calm and unusually honest, but missing real projects, customer voices, people, approved service facts, and live form confirmation |
| Weighted total | **7.7** | `Design × 40% + Usability × 30% + Creativity × 20% + Content × 10%` |

This weighting follows Awwwards’ published Site of the Day evaluation model: Design 40%, Usability 30%, Creativity 20%, and Content 10%. Its developer scorecard separately evaluates Semantics/SEO, Animations/Transitions, Accessibility, WPO, Responsive Design, and Markup/Meta-data. See the [official Awwwards evaluation example](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/computerized-forms) and [Sites of the Year collection](https://www.awwwards.com/websites/sites_of_the_year/).

The SOTY collection includes radically different visual approaches. The useful benchmark is therefore not “add more effects.” It is an unmistakable idea, carried through content, interaction, responsive execution, and technical craft.

## Premium experience diagnosis

| Gate | Score | Finding |

| Looks premium | 8.3 | The first impression, typography, controlled gold, imagery, and spacing are strong |

**Beauty-to-truth gap: 2.5 points.** The largest risk is not visual quality. It is that the design makes a premium promise faster than the current evidence can support it.

**Premium cliff:** the drop begins at the homepage proof chapter, deepens on Projects, Reviews, and About, and becomes operationally explicit on the conditional thank-you screen.

## Jury composition

The audit combined six lenses:

1. Executive art director: coherence, originality, hierarchy, rhythm, and memorability
2. Interaction director: motion purpose, responsiveness, feedback, and signature behavior
3. Conversion strategist: first click, service comprehension, urgency, lead confidence, and drop-off
4. Developer-award reviewer: semantics, accessibility, performance, responsive execution, and metadata
5. Brand skeptic: evidence, claim integrity, visual authenticity, and premium credibility
6. Synthetic homeowner panel: urgent storm caller, planned replacement shopper, skeptical high-income homeowner, insurance-confused homeowner, and mobile first-time visitor

## Homepage, frame by frame

Scores are synthetic weighted estimates, not official Awwwards votes.

### Frame 01 — Header, hero, and first trust boundary

Evidence: [`home-frame-01.png`](../screenshots/audit/home-frame-01.png)

**Score: 8.3**

- Works: bold headline scale, strong house framing, clear local positioning, dual conversion paths, visible phone/license, and confident mobile translation.
- Customer reaction: “This looks more considered than most roofing sites.”
- Award gap: it is still a recognizable premium-contractor composition. The patterned header competes slightly with the logo and navigation.
- Polish directive: reduce header textile contrast by roughly 25%, preserve the hero as the first visual priority, and replace concept imagery with an art-directed real completed home or field sequence.

### Frame 02 — Trust strip and “What brings you here?”

Evidence: [`home-frame-02.png`](../screenshots/audit/home-frame-02.png)

**Score: 7.7**

- Works: problem-first routing is excellent. A homeowner can choose active leak, storm damage, replacement, repair, or exterior work without knowing roofing terminology.
- Customer reaction: “My problem is here; I know where to click.”
- Award gap: five equal boxes read as a competent design-system component rather than a memorable interaction. “Insurance claim experts” carries a broader expectation than the current qualified explanation.
- Polish directive: let the focused or hovered intent reveal a contextual image and next-step preview, while keeping each item a semantic link. Publish trust-strip claims only after operational and legal approval.

### Frame 03 — Dark service gallery

Evidence: [`home-frame-03.png`](../screenshots/audit/home-frame-03.png)

- Customer reaction: “The company seems to understand the whole exterior, not only shingles.”
- Award gap: four equal cards and concept-driven images keep the section in polished territory.
- Polish directive: feature one priority service at a wider span, vary the rhythm, and replace every image with matched real project or team photography.

## Synthetic homeowner jury

### Urgent storm-damage caller

- Wins: immediate phone access, storm path, mobile sticky actions, clear carrier boundary.
- Loses: no hours, after-hours policy, response expectation, tarping/mitigation scope, or confirmed service radius.
- Predicted behavior: calls quickly; leaves if no answer or if urgency is not acknowledged.

### Planned roof-replacement shopper

- Wins: “planning a replacement” routing, service education, calm process.
- Loses: no roof-system comparison, material/manufacturer specifics, real projects, written warranty, or financing terms.
- Predicted behavior: explores, then opens Projects/Reviews and comparison-shops elsewhere.

### Skeptical premium homeowner

- Wins: hero, typography, restraint, lack of invented proof.
- Loses: concept imagery, no people, no completed work, no sourced customer voice, and public prelaunch language.
- Predicted behavior: impressed for five seconds, unconvinced at the third click.

### Insurance-confused homeowner

- Wins: storm guidance and explicit statement that coverage decisions belong to the carrier.
- Loses: “Insurance claim experts” can imply broader representation than later copy supports.
- Predicted behavior: calls with expectations the site may need to correct.

### Mobile first-time visitor

- Wins: readable hero, direct call/inspection actions, usable menu, no horizontal overflow.
- Loses: sticky-action/menu duplication, long lower homepage, no early proof, and oversized footer.
- Predicted behavior: converts quickly if already motivated; proof-seeking users exit before reaching the bottom.

## Hard score caps

These conditions prevent a credible Site of the Year or public-launch recommendation even if the visuals improve:

1. No production form delivery and no server-confirmed success state
2. No real project case studies
3. No approved customer-review excerpts on-site
4. No real owner/team/company photography or accountable company story
5. Public prelaunch language about replacement images, pending verification, and conditional endpoints
6. Unapproved or incompletely scoped claims, especially free inspections, insurance expertise, response speed, service area, warranty, and financing
7. No signature interaction that turns “Built Above Standard” into an experience only this company could own

## Priority execution plan

### P0 — Make every premium promise true

1. Connect a secure form/CRM endpoint with validation, sanitization, spam protection, rate limiting, consent, routing ownership, and a tested response process.
2. Replace conditional thank-you language with a server-confirmed receipt, reference number, response expectation, and urgent fallback.
3. Capture and publish three verified projects: storm restoration, roof replacement, and repair or gutters/exteriors.
4. Add approved review excerpts and real founder/team content.
5. Approve exact business hours, urgent-response policy, service area, insurance-assistance scope, warranty, financing, and supported trust-strip language.
6. Remove all customer-facing build commentary after the real content replaces it.

### P1 — Build the one winning interaction

Create an accessible “Above Standard” roof-system story from one real project. A sticky real roof/detail image should reveal five documented decisions:

1. Decking and substrate
2. Flashing and water management
3. Ventilation and whole-system fit
4. Property protection and communication
5. Closeout verification

Each decision should be keyboard-operable, useful without animation, preserved in reduced-motion mode, and connected to the verified case study and inspection action.

This is the highest-leverage move because it raises Design, Creativity, Content, trust, and conversion at the same time.

### P2 — Recompose the narrative

1. Move one real project and one sourced review into the first two homepage screens.
2. Merge reputation, About, and service-area content into one local-proof chapter.
3. Cut homepage length by 20–25%.
4. Replace repeated five-column grids with a featured service, connected process timeline, and evidence-led layouts.
5. Give each service page one unique, service-specific proof or decision module.
6. Carry intent and service selections into the contact form.

### P3 — Refine motion, mobile, and performance

1. Quiet the header/footer pattern without losing the proprietary brand asset.
2. Resolve menu/sticky-action duplication and add bottom clearance.
3. Shorten and simplify the mobile footer.
4. Add responsive modern image formats and self-hosted font subsets.
5. Run production Lighthouse/Core Web Vitals, browser matrix, real-device, screen-reader, zoom, and forced-colors testing.

### P4 — Validate with real people

Recruit at least two people per customer type and give them uncoached tasks:

1. “Water is entering your home after a storm at 9 p.m. Find what to do and whether this company can help.”
2. “Decide whether this contractor belongs on your three-company roof-replacement shortlist.”
3. “Prove this is a legitimate premium local contractor.”
4. “Explain what the contractor does versus what the insurance carrier decides.”
5. “Request help on your phone and tell me whether the request was received.”

Launch-quality gates:

- At least 80% choose the correct first path without assistance
- Median urgent-help time under 30 seconds
- Zero participants believe the contractor decides insurance coverage
- Every form participant knows whether submission succeeded
- Average trust of at least 4/5 after Projects, Reviews, and About
- No critical keyboard, screen-reader, zoom, responsive, or form-delivery failure

## What not to add

- Autoplay audio
- Decorative WebGL or parallax that slows urgent tasks
- More generic black-and-gold cards
- Fabricated stars, review counts, locations, projects, badges, or response times
- Animation without information value
- A mascot placement that substitutes personality for proof

## Final recommendation

Do not chase more spectacle yet. The site’s visual foundation is already good enough to support award-level work. The shortest credible path is:

**real proof → confirmed operations → signature roof-system story → narrative compression → production validation**

After those steps, the site could credibly compete in the strong Site of the Day range. Site of the Year should remain an aspiration until the experience becomes as proprietary and evidenced in the middle and final clicks as it already feels in the first five seconds.
