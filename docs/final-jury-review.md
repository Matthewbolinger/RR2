# Final Jury Review

Review date: July 30, 2026

Evidence reviewed:

- Production build and automated integrity checks
- 390px mobile hero and menu
- 768px tablet homepage and all three quote-form steps
- 1440px desktop hero, full homepage, service page, services, standard pillars, contact, thank-you, and footer
- Browser smoke tests for menu, form states, overflow, and 404 behavior
- Claims, asset, accessibility, performance, and launch documentation

## 1. Executive Creative Director — 9.2/10

The site translates the preferred board into a disciplined architectural system rather than reproducing the collage. Typography, cream/dark rhythm, grid discipline, photography, controlled rules, and restrained gold feel coherent and premium.

Improvement: replace the temporary typography-only wordmark and generic pattern with approved production identity assets.

## 2. Roofing Homeowner — 9.0/10

Service paths, active-leak and storm routing, phone access, process explanation, verification boundaries, and mobile sticky actions make the next step clear. The site is reassuring without minimizing the problem.

Improvement: add real local projects and approved homeowner feedback for stronger pre-call proof.

## 3. Conversion Strategist — 8.8/10

The quote CTA is prominent, the phone is always reachable, intent routing is useful, and the three-step form captures the property, project, and contact data required for automated lead routing without presenting a long wall of fields. Conversion events wait for endpoint confirmation.

Blocker: the production intake endpoint, CRM routing, spam protection, duplicate controls, and real receipt tests are not configured. The current honest recovery state prevents false submission claims.

## 4. Home-Services Operator — 8.7/10

The inspection-to-closeout process is credible and maintainable. The copy distinguishes observed conditions, scope, and coverage decisions. Centralized data makes service and claim changes practical.

Blocker: exact service availability, operating hours, warranty, financing, insurance, team ownership, and response standards need operational approval.

## 5. Local SEO Strategist — 8.8/10

The build includes service architecture, a verified Barrington page, unique metadata, canonicals, sitemap, robots, schema, internal links, and honest location expansion rules.

Blocker: local proof is too thin for broader geographic expansion. Add real case studies, approved reviews, and a verified service-area list before creating more city pages.

## 6. Accessibility Specialist — 9.0/10

Semantic landmarks, one H1, skip link, native disclosure controls, keyboard mobile menu, focus restoration, visible focus, labeled form steps, inline errors, live status, reduced motion, and tested overflow provide a strong baseline.

Improvement: complete a VoiceOver/NVDA pass, automated scan, 200% zoom review, and forced-colors review on the production host.

## 7. Performance Engineer — 8.9/10

Static HTML, no framework runtime, about 17.5 KB uncompressed/5.1 KB gzip application JavaScript, no configured third-party scripts, stable dimensions, lazy loading, and asset caching provide a lean foundation.

Improvement: add responsive AVIF/WebP sources, self-host font subsets, and run production Lighthouse. Two below-fold JPEGs remain above the preferred 180 KB budget.

## 8. Brand Skeptic — 8.8/10

The mascot is not used as borrowed trust, the generated images are disclosed as editorial, proof is not invented, and “Built Above Standard” is substantiated through concrete operating behaviors.

Blocker: the brand still needs real people, real completed work, approved supporting identity assets, and an approved restrained mascot asset to feel unmistakably proprietary rather than launch-ready editorial.

## Jury conclusion

The website implementation is suitable for stakeholder review and integration. It is not approved for public production launch until the launch checklist’s brand, proof, form, verification, legal, and hosting blockers are resolved.

Scores below 9 are tied to explicit content, integration, or asset blockers rather than hidden behind inflated ratings.
